    The Challenge

    Wound Care Today Is Reactive. Patients Pay the Price.

    Every day, clinicians make critical wound care decisions using information that reflects the past rather than predicts the future. By the time deterioration is visible, complications are already underway.

    Delayed Visual Detection

    Wound deterioration begins subcutaneous days before visible surface warning signs appear, causing late clinical intervention.

    Preventable Complications

    Advanced pressure ulcers and surgical site breakdowns cause avoidable hospital readmissions and lengthy patient setbacks.

    Heavy Documentation Burden

    Manual paperwork and charting inconsistencies consume valuable nursing hours and expose organizations to survey deficiencies.

    Siloed Facility Oversight

    Clinical directors and executive leaders lack centralized visibility into healing trajectories across multiple facilities and care units.

    "The issue is that traditional systems only show what has already happened. They rarely help clinicians identify what happens next."

    — Clinical Leadership Perspective in Long-Term Care

    The Solution

    Most Platforms Document Wounds. Wound-IQ Detects Risk.

    By analyzing longitudinal clinical data, wound imagery, nutritional biomarkers, and healing velocity, Wound-IQ empowers care teams to intervene days before deterioration escalates. That difference changes outcomes.

    How It Works

    Intelligence Embedded into Clinical Workflows

    Zero operational friction. Wound-IQ functions seamlessly in the background with standard devices and familiar assessment protocols already used by your nurses.

    Step 01

    Capture

    Clinicians complete standard wound assessments and imagery using familiar workflows and existing mobile devices.

    Step 02

    Analyze

    Wound-IQ continuously analyzes perimeter changes, depth indicators, and longitudinal healing trends.

    AI-powered early-warning models spot pressure trajectories associated with impending deterioration.

    Step 04

    Step 05

    Act
